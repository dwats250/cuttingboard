REASONING

The strongest case for Option B is real and I want to state it at full strength
before rejecting it: A's annotation is a self-declaration the CI guard admits it
cannot verify (PRD-276's own STATED LIMITATION), which sits badly with PRD-198
invariant 2; the design took five connector rounds and 21 findings to stabilize
and still has a live silent-bypass at the reviewed SHA (3688912753); and VISION
says this is not a regulated-infrastructure project. If deleting the
payload/pointer distinction deleted the defect surface, B would win. It does
not. Of the 11 matcher/parsing defects, 8 live in CLASS parsing, LANE parsing,
FILES extraction, and document seeding — machinery any mechanized version of the
rule needs, Option B included (B's guard must still know a PRD's CLASS to pick
the right HIGH-RISK FILES set, read its LANE, and extract its FILES). Only 3
defects are in the annotation matcher B would delete. B keeps the gauntlet and
gives up the carve-out.

Second, Option B is not a stable resting point — it is the design this branch
started from, and the connector's own P1 findings are what pushed it to A. The
pre-276 rule WAS Option B, and it was simultaneously unenforced and
unfollowable: `docs/PRD_MICRO_TEMPLATE.md` itself emits `docs/PRD_REGISTRY.md`
and `docs/PROJECT_STATE.md` into every FILES section, so under B the canonical
micro template is invalid for the entire GOVERNANCE class — every governance PRD
intersects by construction. That is why 5 of 21 governance PRDs violated the old
rule without anyone noticing until connector thread 3687825284. A rule that the
repo's own required template forces you to break is worse than either a complex
rule or no rule. Re-adopting B means either every governance PRD carries
HIGH-RISK forever (see next paragraph) or the template's two bookkeeping lines
get deleted and the registry touch goes silent — which is exactly the omission
evasion the connector flagged as P1 (3688464618): silence becomes cheaper than a
false declaration, and nothing mechanical sees the edit at all.

Third, B's cost is understated in the framing I was given. The waiver sentence
is genuinely cheap — but B also forecloses the micro template (locked to
`LANE: MICRO`), so ~2.5 governance PRDs/month (7 in the PRD-121..275 span) pay
full-template HIGH-RISK ceremony for row bookkeeping. Worse are the two semantic
costs: HIGH-RISK dilution — Dustin's merge attention is the scarce resource the
lane axis protects, and labeling a one-line pointer move HIGH-RISK trains him to
skim the label — and waiver routinization: PRD-242 designed the `SECOND-MODEL:`
sentence as a positive act by the merger, never boilerplate. A rule that makes
the sentence rote monthly paperwork erodes precisely the gate-skip control
PRD-242 closed. This is PRD-229's incident replayed in slow motion: twelve
cosmetic PRDs paid full HIGH-RISK ceremony in two days, and the repo built
ceremony tiering specifically to stop that. B partially un-builds it.

Finally, the review-cost argument for B collapses on inspection. Under CLAUDE.md
GOV-1, every PR already gets exactly one fresh-context structured review — so B
buys no additional diff-reader over A+R5 for the case that matters. The delta
really is the disposition sentence plus template ceremony plus semantics, and
A's marginal complexity over B, post-hardening, is about 20 lines of code (one
regex, one tuple, one branch) and a vocabulary that must stay synchronized — a
real but bounded cost, now pinned by 14 tests. What the authoring agent got
wrong in recommending B: treating the seven rounds as evidence against the
payload/pointer rule, when the defect ledger shows most of the bleeding came
from mechanizing ANY lane guard in this repo (compare the multi-round hardening
history of `_scan_doc_status_lines` for PRD-269 — same gauntlet, no annotation
vocabulary in sight), and treating "one waiver sentence" as the whole cost of B
while omitting the template foreclosure and the two control-erosion effects.
